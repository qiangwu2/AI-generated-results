VERDICT

For the revised 34-page manuscript reviewed here, this fresh recheck found
no fatal mathematical error or unresolved substantive gap in the proof of
the initial support interval. The written manuscript still needs the local
corrections below. Their justifications use hypotheses or estimates already
available in the manuscript; they do not require a new theorem about the
Parisi minimizer.

This is a scoped mathematical audit, not a claim that the file contains
literally zero errors or a formal verification of every assertion.

The theorem concerns zero-field SK at each fixed finite beta > 1, with
xi(q)=beta^2 q^2/2. It asserts existence of q_beta > 0 such that

    [0,q_beta] is contained in supp(mu_beta),
    mu_beta({0})=0,
    beta*u_xx(0,0)=1,
    u_xxxx(0,0)<0,
    the CDF is C-infinity on [0,q_beta).

It does not establish that the entire support is a single interval. This
verdict applies to this revised version, rather than automatically to all
earlier files bearing the July 14 date. A printed date alone does not date
each correction historically.

FRESH CHECKS AND CLAIM-EVIDENCE RECORD

The recheck was divided into independent analytic, slope/first-gap, and
conclusion-chain reviews, alongside the main review. The independent
reviewers were instructed to derive their findings before reading prior
audit conclusions.

1. Finite-temperature analytic inputs: CHECKED.
   Proposition 2.1 matches Auffinger-Chen Propositions 1-2. Appendix
   Proposition 5, equations (66) and (68), supplies derivative constants
   depending only on xi for atomic profiles, and Proposition 1 passes
   spatial bounds and convergence to arbitrary probability measures.
   Positive curvature is available, not merely strict convexity in the
   abstract sense. The fixed finite-beta bound is valid. The false
   same-cutoff bound in the separate zero-temperature draft is not used.

2. Stochastic identities for arbitrary profiles: CHECKED.
   Uniform derivative convergence and synchronous coupling give diffusion
   convergence. Bounded integrands justify the stochastic limits. The
   constructed approximations preserve a prescribed constant-profile
   interval, so the higher identities on support gaps are applicable.

3. Endpoint expansions and closed cone: CHECKED, with local corrections
   A, B and C below.
   The weighted half-line barrier controls the stated growth uniformly
   over atomic partitions. The coefficient ODEs preserve a positive
   leading endpoint coefficient. The fixed-parameter evolution supplies
   the spatial and mixed estimates needed for the endpoint traces.
   The maximum-principle sign order is noncircular. The closure handles
   c=1 directly and c=0 by positive-parameter approximations followed by
   a final decrease to zero. No zero-parameter Cole-Hopf operation is
   needed in that construction.

4. Strict generalized first-gap inequality: CHECKED.
   The logarithmic weight derivative is -(N+z)/C. The time derivatives,
   covariance identity, strict covariance term, tail bound 0<=tau<=Cz,
   and the inequality reversal in (72) have the needed signs. Gaussian
   endpoint decay justifies the integrations and differentiations on
   compact interior time intervals. Strictness is proved directly for
   the limiting profile, rather than inferred from a limit of strict
   inequalities. The moment comparison gives q(t)<t*q'(0).

5. Origin accumulation, marginality and no atom: CHECKED.
   The rescaling t=beta^2*q_* is consistent with both the balance identity
   and support stability. The isolated-origin assumption contradicts
   beta^2*u_xx(0,0)^2<=1. Accumulating support points imply marginality.
   The no-atom expansion is Gamma(s)=s-beta*mu({0})*s^2+o(s^2).

6. Strict fourth derivative and exclusion of small gaps: CHECKED.
   At parameter zero, nonnegativity and monotonicity of C^(3/2)*J force
   J(0)>0, since J(0)=0 would make C constant in contradiction with its
   endpoint behavior. On every support gap,

     Gamma'''=beta^6 E[R^2-12m*C*D^2+6m^2*C^4].

   The bracket tends to u_xxxx(0,0)^2>0 through all sufficiently small
   gap points. Hence Gamma' is strictly convex on each such gap.
   Strict Hermite-Hadamard contradicts endpoint self-consistency and
   stability. The contradiction remains valid with atoms at gap
   endpoints because Gamma' is continuous.

7. Smoothness conclusion: CHECKED, with citation correction D below.
   Auffinger-Chen Theorem 2(ii) supplies exactly C-infinity on [0,q_beta).

LOCAL CORRECTIONS REQUIRED IN THE WRITTEN FILE

A. Physical page 11, Section 4 before (25)-(26); also Appendix R.5.
   The general setup must say smooth with psi''>0. Evenness and strict
   convexity alone do not ensure smooth slope coordinates. For example,

     psi(x)=log(cosh x)-(1/2)*tanh(x)^2,
     psi'(x)=tanh(x)^3,
     C(B)=3*|B|^(2/3)*(1-|B|^(2/3)),

   gives a smooth even strictly convex function whose C is not C1 at
   zero. Actual Parisi profiles satisfy psi''>0 by Proposition 2.1,
   and the fixed-flow hypotheses in Lemma 4.4 and Appendix A explicitly
   require this stronger condition. Thus this false general formulation
   does not invalidate the theorem's applications.

B. Physical pages 24-25, Appendix A, (104)-(108).
   The statement that the parameter-dependent inverse function theorem
   directly gives bounded Y_{r,delta^4} omits a derivative-budget step.
   Differentiating F(r,Y)=delta at that order introduces F_{y^5}*Y_r.
   The already-established pure spatial remainder estimates give
   F_{y^5}=O(y^{-1}); F_r=O(y) and F_y>=c>0 give Y_r=O(y).
   Their product is bounded. The same cancellation supplies the mixed
   derivative bound for A in (107). This fills the omitted justification
   using existing estimates, without an additional regularity assumption.

C. Physical page 26, Corollary A.4.
   The allowed operations appear to permit decreasing the parameter to
   zero and subsequently applying T_{a,r}, although (36) defines this
   transform only for a>0. Restrict the final decrease to zero to be the
   last operation. That restricted statement is sufficient for every
   application in Proposition 4.2, including its c=0 approximation.

D. Physical page 21, proof of Theorem 1.1.
   The description of Auffinger-Chen Theorem 2(ii) reverses the endpoint
   direction. The source gives smoothness on [a,b), including the left
   endpoint. It does not assert smoothness at b from the left. The stated
   conclusion on [0,q_beta) is nevertheless exactly supported.

Minor presentation detail: the maximum-principle proof in Lemma A.1 can
be applied first on a smaller time strip and then passed to its terminal
time by continuity, avoiding a terminal derivative not explicitly
included in the abstract hypotheses.
